Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 October 2026

FoodSpot (“we”) is an Android app, made by ftwco., that pins restaurants you discover in Instagram and YouTube food reels on a map. This policy explains what data FoodSpot uses, where it goes, and the choices you have.

In short: you log in with your email or Google so your saved spots are kept in your FoodSpot account and follow you to a new phone. We store only what that needs. We have no ads, and we don’t sell or share your data. You can delete your account and everything in it from the app at any time.

1. Your account

  • Email address: to log you in. When you log in with a code, we email you a 6-digit code. It’s stored only as a scrambled (hashed) value and expires after 10 minutes.
  • Google sign-in (optional): your Google account ID, verified email and name. We ask Google only for these basic details (openid email profile).
  • Sign-in limits: to stop abuse, we count code requests per email address and per IP address for an hour.
  • Your login stays active on a phone until you log out. You can log out of all phones at once.

Google user data. If you sign in with Google, we use your name, email address and Google account ID only to create your FoodSpot account, log you in and keep your spots in sync. We don’t use Google user data for advertising, don’t sell it, and don’t transfer it to anyone except as needed to run FoodSpot (our server host, Cloudflare). FoodSpot’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. You can remove FoodSpot’s access at any time in your Google Account permissions.

2. Your saved spots

  • What: restaurant names, addresses, map positions, your notes, and the link and creator of the reel each came from.
  • Where: in your FoodSpot account on our server (Cloudflare), so they sync between your phones, and as a copy on each phone you’re logged in on, so the map works offline. Logging out removes the copy from that phone; the spots stay in your account.
  • Only you can see your saved spots. Nobody else can, unless you share a spot yourself.
  • We keep them until you delete them or delete your account.

3. Data that stays on your phone

  • Your location: if you allow it, FoodSpot uses it to show where you are on the map. Your exact location never leaves your phone and isn’t stored in your account. When you share a reel, FoodSpot may send an approximate position to the place-search service below so that nearby restaurants rank first.
  • Creator profile cache: public profile details (name, photo, follower counts) of creators whose reels you saved, kept so their profiles open quickly.

4. Services FoodSpot uses

To do its job, the app makes requests to these services directly from your phone. They see your IP address and the request, under their own privacy policies:

  • Instagram and YouTube: to read the public caption, title and creator of a reel you share, and public creator profile pages.
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim and Photon (komoot): to turn restaurant names and addresses into map positions. Search text, and sometimes an approximate position, is sent.
  • OpenFreeMap: map tiles. unpkg and Google Fonts: the map’s code and fonts.
  • Google Maps: when you tap “Let’s go”, the destination opens in Google Maps.
  • Cloudflare: runs our server (your account and spots) and this website.
  • Resend: sends the login-code emails (it receives your email address and the code).
  • GitHub: hosts app downloads and older shared-spot links.

5. Sharing a spot

A shared spot link contains the spot itself (name, address, map position, reel link and creator), encoded in the part of the link after “#”. The link itself isn’t stored on our server. Anyone you send it to can see those details.

6. Creators who claim their profile

Creators can claim their FoodSpot profile and connect their other social accounts by signing in with Instagram or Google (YouTube). When they do:

  • We receive their account’s ID, username/handle and display name, and store them on our server (Cloudflare) with the time they connected, so every FoodSpot user can see the connected accounts.
  • We use the platform’s access token once, during sign-in, to read those details, and then discard it. We don’t store access tokens, and we don’t post, message or read anything else.
  • Permissions requested: Instagram instagram_business_basic (profile basics of a professional account); Google youtube.readonly (to identify your YouTube channel).
  • A claimed profile is linked to the FoodSpot account that claimed it. Only that creator can connect or disconnect accounts on it.
  • A creator can disconnect an account or remove their profile at any time in the app (profile → “You manage this profile” → Options), or see Delete your data.

FoodSpot’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. FoodSpot uses YouTube API Services: by connecting a YouTube account you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google’s data use is covered by the Google Privacy Policy. You can revoke FoodSpot’s access at any time from your Google Account permissions or Instagram’s “Apps and websites” settings.

7. Deleting your data

In the app: My spots → your initial (top right) → Delete account. This immediately and permanently deletes your account, all your saved spots and any creator profile you claimed. You can also email us; see Delete your data.

8. Children

FoodSpot isn’t directed at children under 13, and creator sign-in is for adults with professional social media accounts.

9. Changes and contact

We’ll update this page if anything changes, and change the date above. Questions or requests: privacy@foodspot.app.
